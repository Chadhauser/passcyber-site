Serviced office, co-working space, hot desks — whose firewall is it anyway?

Thousands of small organisations work from serviced offices, co-working spaces and coffee shops where somebody else runs the network. The firewall questions in Cyber Essentials look impossible from that chair — they aren't. The standard already has an answer: when you don't control the network, the boundary moves to the device. Here's how that works and exactly what to put.

Reading time 3 minutesServiced offices · co-working · remote-first teams

The principle: untrusted network = software firewalls

Cyber Essentials treats a network you don't control exactly like a home network or café Wi-Fi: untrusted. The protection the standard wants is then the software firewall on each device — Windows Defender Firewall, macOS's firewall — switched on, configured, and not full of forgotten “allow” rules. You are not marked down for renting a desk; you're assessed on the devices you actually control.

How to answer the firewall questions

  • Describe reality: “we operate from a serviced office / co-working space; the network is managed by the provider and treated as untrusted; all organisational devices rely on enabled, configured software firewalls.” Assessors read that as competence, not weakness.
  • Check every device: firewall on, no blanket inbound allows, password required to change settings. Ten minutes per machine.
  • Remote access: if anything is reachable from the internet (a NAS, a server, remote desktop), that's where boundary questions get real — in a serviced office the honest answer is usually “nothing is”, and cloud services carry the load instead (which is where the MFA rule takes over).

The co-working checklist

  • Software firewall enabled and configured on every laptop — the core answer.
  • Devices don't trust the office network: no shared drives mapped across it, printing via the provider's approved route.
  • Screen-lock on idle everywhere — shared space makes physical basics part of the story.
  • VPN if you want belt-and-braces on open Wi-Fi — good practice, not a certification requirement.
  • Provider-supplied kit (a meeting-room PC you actually use for work) — either keep organisational data off it entirely or accept it into scope; there's no half-way.
Cyber Essentials Plus in a shared space

Plus adds an on-site/remote technical audit — still workable from a serviced office, provided each device stands on its own: software firewall, hardened configuration, supported OS. The assessor tests your machines, not your landlord's switch cupboard.

Net position: a serviced-office organisation with clean devices and MFA on its cloud services is often easier to certify than a traditional office dragging an elderly server — the estate is smaller and the boundary is honest. The trap isn't the desk you rent; it's the one unmanaged laptop nobody checked.
